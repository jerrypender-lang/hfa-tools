Know your numbers, save your life

Three numbers.
One conversation.

Pick a number to see what it is, why it matters, and the one question to bring to your next checkup.

What it is

The force of your blood pushing against your artery walls. It’s written as two numbers, one over the other.

Why it matters

High blood pressure often has no symptoms at all. You can feel fine and still be at risk—the only way to know is to check.

Ask your doctor

“What’s my blood pressure today—and what should it be for me?”

What it is

A blood test that shows your average blood sugar over the past two to three months.

Why it matters

It’s one of the main ways doctors find and track diabetes and prediabetes—often before you notice anything.

Ask your doctor

“What’s my A1C, and has it changed since my last test?”

What it is

Fats in your blood. Some kinds can build up in your arteries over time.

Why it matters

High cholesterol usually has no symptoms, but it raises your risk of heart attack and stroke.

Ask your doctor

“What are my cholesterol numbers—and what do they mean for my heart?”

General health education, not medical advice. Only your doctor can tell you what your numbers mean for you.
