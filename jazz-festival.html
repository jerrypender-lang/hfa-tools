The Lowcountry Jazz Festival · Since 2009

Culture and care go hand in hand.

A family doctor and his daughter turned a Labor Day weekend of jazz into scholarships for future healthcare workers. Today it’s a Charleston tradition, and the main source of funding for Closing the Gap in Healthcare.

01The why

Not enough doctors who looked like the patients.

Dr. Thaddeus Bell was one of the first African American graduates of the Medical University of South Carolina. Decades into his career as a family physician, he kept seeing the same problem: there weren’t enough Black physicians serving rural and underserved communities, and for many talented students, the barrier was money.

He had already spent years teaching people how to understand their own health. Now he wanted to help the next generation of caregivers get through school, and he needed a way to raise the money year after year that the whole community would show up for.

The answer was music.

A weekend of world-class jazz, every Labor Day, in Charleston.
02A father and daughter

Built by two Bells.

Dr. Bell founded the Lowcountry Jazz Festival in 2009 with his daughter, Tonisha Bell-Alston. Tonisha was also instrumental in building Closing the Gap in Healthcare. It was her idea to take her father’s health tips online through the Health-E-Newsletter.

Tonisha passed away from cancer in 2015. Her purpose lives on in every scholarship the festival funds.

In loving memory of Tonisha Bell-Alston

04Where the money goes

Every ticket does three jobs.

Buy a seat for the music, and you’re also paying for a student’s tuition and a health talk in someone’s neighborhood.

01

Scholarships for future caregivers

From the very first festival, ticket sales have funded scholarships for students working toward careers in health care: future doctors, nurses, pharmacists and health professionals. Updated eligibility will be announced here.

Admit oneFuture caregiver
02

Health education that makes sense

The festival is the main source of funding for Closing the Gap in Healthcare. It pays for Dr. Bell Says videos, barbershop health talks, radio tips and community events across South Carolina.
